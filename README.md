# 🎤 Presentation Script — First Two Slides

---

## Slide 1: Title Slide — *"The Architecture of Influence"*

> **Subtitle:** Diagnosing Money Power as a Structural Pathology in Modern Democracies
> **Tagline:** A Systemic Analysis of the 2024 Indian Elections, Global Campaign Finance Paradigms, and Multi-Layered Policy Reform.

### What to Say:

> *"Good morning/afternoon everyone.*
>
> *The title of our presentation is **'The Architecture of Influence'** — and the word 'architecture' is deliberate here. We're not just talking about a few corrupt politicians or one or two scandals. We're talking about an **entire system** — a carefully constructed architecture — through which money power has become a **structural disease** inside modern democracies.*
>
> *The fundamental promise of democracy is political equality — **one person, one vote**. But what happens when the cost of contesting an election becomes so astronomically high that only the ultra-rich can afford to run? What happens when corporations can pour billions into anonymous donations and buy policy influence behind closed doors? What happens when political parties start choosing candidates not based on their integrity, but based on their **bank balance and muscle power**?*
>
> *That's exactly what this presentation unpacks. We've done a **systemic analysis** grounded in three pillars:*
> - *First — the **2024 Indian general elections**, the most expensive electoral exercise in world history.*
> - *Second — a **global comparison** of how different democracies handle campaign finance — specifically the United States and Brazil.*
> - *And third — concrete, **multi-layered policy reforms** that could begin to fix this broken system.*
>
> *So let's begin by looking at the sheer scale of money we're dealing with."*

---

## Slide 2: *"The Financial Scale of the 2024 Democratic Exercise"*

> **Key data points on this slide:**
> - ₹1.35 lakh crore total expenditure (projected by CMS)
> - More than double the ₹55,000–60,000 crore spent in 2019
> - India at \$16 Billion vs US at \$14.4 Billion
> - Per-voter cost: ₹1,400 across 96.6 crore eligible voters

### What to Say:

> *"Now, let's talk numbers — because the numbers here are staggering.*
>
> *The 2024 Lok Sabha elections, according to the Centre for Media Studies, cost an estimated **₹1.35 lakh crore** — that's roughly **\$16 billion**. To put that in perspective, this is **more than double** the ₹55,000 to 60,000 crore spent in the 2019 general elections. The cost of Indian democracy literally doubled in just five years.*
>
> *And here's the part that should really make you stop and think — look at the comparison on the right side. The 2020 United States Presidential and Congressional elections — arguably the most high-profile elections in the world — cost an estimated **\$14.4 billion**. India's 2024 elections **surpassed even that**. India has now overtaken the world's oldest continuous democracy in the sheer cost of running an election.*
>
> *Now, break it down to the individual voter level. With **96.6 crore eligible voters**, this comes out to approximately **₹1,400 spent per voter**. And this isn't just about official campaign advertising — this figure includes pre-election activities months before the official announcement, massive political rallies, transportation logistics, hiring of field workers and digital influencers, extensive media campaigns, and even the Election Commission's own operational costs, which account for roughly 10 to 15 percent of the total.*
>
> *But here's the critical structural problem. Under the law, the Election Commission caps **individual candidate** spending at ₹95 lakh for larger states and ₹75 lakh for smaller ones. However — and this is the loophole that enables everything — **political parties themselves have no spending limit whatsoever**. Section 77 of the Representation of the People Act was specifically amended to ensure that party spending doesn't count toward the candidate's limit. So while the candidate is micro-managed, the party behind them is a **conduit for unlimited, unregulated capital**.*
>
> *And who provides that capital? That takes us into the world of corporate funding and the Electoral Bond Scheme — which we'll cover next."*
